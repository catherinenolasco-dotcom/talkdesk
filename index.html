Case studies

Four projects, start to finish.

The numbers above came from real work. Here is what each project looked like.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI, Feb 2026 - Apr 2026
Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process that created real audit risk. Early feedback was that a formal form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

Designed a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that requires documentation and approval before any HRIS change, and brought every stakeholder into the design. Built Notion-based New Hire and Offboarding workflows where every task has an owner, reminders go out in Slack and email, and approvals route across Payroll, IT, People, and the hiring manager.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding that starts within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI, Feb 2026 - Apr 2026
Challenge

A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India, with agreements to draft and every step to coordinate with Payroll and IT.

What I did

Built AI-enabled severance and cost modeling with Claude and Gemini that calculated each employee's severance amount, COBRA cost, and COBRA months owed, then drafted the agreement. Tracked every step on a shared spreadsheet that Payroll and IT worked from during execution.

Result

Leadership saw RIF costs for the first time, and agreement prep went from 20 minutes per employee to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

ClaudeGemini

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell, Dec 2018 - Jan 2026
Challenge

On day one there was no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in disconnected spreadsheets, and employee questions had no tracked channel beyond email or word of mouth.

What I did

Built a centralized, documented backbone: a 20+ procedure SOP library with named owners, backup coverage, and annual reviews; ASKPeople, a Jira and Confluence ticketing system with tiered SLAs; and Sapling as the HR single source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected to Greenhouse, Slack, and Gmail through Zapier.

Result

Scaled from 3 countries to 5, passed 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, cut approval cycles from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and resolved 5-10 tickets a day in 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

How I would start as Head of People Operations and Rewards.

A proposed plan built around the six-month picture in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet People leaders, Business Partners, Talent, Programs, Systems, Rewards, and business leaders to hear where work slows down.
  • Map how work flows across People today: where it sits, who owns it, which systems touch it, and where handoffs break.
  • Baseline volumes, cycle times, manual effort, and audit posture.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Draft the People operating model with decision rights, intake, and a single roadmap that shows capacity.
  • Choose two or three high-volume transactional workflows to redesign around AI and automation, each with an owner and an approval path.
  • Review benefits, rewards programs, and vendor spend against ROI.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Launch the first redesigned workflows with an audit trail and a clear owner for each step.
  • Start a monthly planning rhythm that decides what to accelerate, simplify, automate, defer, or stop.
  • Report baseline against current results to leadership.
By month 6

One connected system

  • Clear decision rights and seamless handoffs across People.
  • Leaders can see capacity, investments, and outcomes.
  • Manual and transactional work is materially reduced, and programs are simpler, faster, and measurable.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with your People team and business leaders.
